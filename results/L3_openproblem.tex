\paragraph{An open problem: the last $O(1/K^{2})$.}
Corollary~\ref{cor:greedybudget} and Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} leave a
sandwich.  Within the class $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{lin}}$ of deterministic
algorithms operating on predictive greedy's own budget, at most $nK$
queries of size at most $K$, the best achievable worst-case ratio at error
level $\eta$ lies in the interval
$[\rho_K(\eta),\,\min\{U_K(\eta),1/\eta\}]$, whose width at fixed
$\eta$ is $c'(\eta)/K^{2}+O(1/K^{3})$
(Remark~\ref{rem:greedybudget}).  At $K=8$ and $\eta=2$ the interval reads
$[0.4216,\,0.4242]$, against the unrestricted-query ceiling $1/\eta=0.5$.
For $\eta\ge K$ the interval is a point and predictive greedy is exactly
optimal within the class.  For $1<\eta<K$ and $n\ge4K^{5}$ we do not know whether the lower
end moves: in that range we are not aware of any algorithm in
$\mathcal A_{\mathrm{lin}}$
whose worst-case ratio exceeds $\rho_K(\eta)$.  The quantifier on $n$ is
necessary: for small $n$ the budget $nK$ admits exhaustive search over all
$K$-subsets (at $n=4$, $K=2$, $\eta=3/2$ it guarantees $2/3=1/\eta$, above
$\rho_2=3/5$), so no greedy-optimality reading survives there.
% M2.3 (J5 section 11 counterexample, recomputed exactly:
% results/M0_counterexamples.py part 2).  Two natural linear-budget candidates provide no counterexample at the
exact-LP level ($K\le3$, $n\le7$, adversarial ties): choosing the best
$K$-subset of a top-$(K{+}1)$ shortlist by predicted values collapses to a
one-element guarantee, far below $\rho_K$ in all twenty solved
configurations, while predictive greedy followed by a single swap pass on
$\tilde f$ matches $\rho_K$ exactly at $n=2K$ and falls strictly below it
at every solved larger-$n$ configuration, all four error levels at $K=3$,
$n=7$ included.
% Source: results/L2_linear_candidates.md sections 0, 2, 5 (5.1.1: the two
% formerly open K=3 n=7 cells were exhausted on 2026-09-11, exact values
% 31/51 and 37/69, both strictly below rho_3); ledger T11.
Partial enumeration, which improves the finite-$K$ constant in the
exact-oracle setting of Nemhauser and Wolsey and, unlike the two candidates
above, does beat $\rho_3$ at $n=2K$, is finite-parameter evidence in the
same direction rather than a way out: at $R=1$, $K=3$ its exact worst case
falls strictly below $\rho_3$ already at $n=7,8$ for $\eta\in\{2,\,2.5\}$,
the gain of enumerating a starting element being outpaced by the loss of
selecting the endpoint by $\tilde f$.  A
construction direction with finite exhaustive support
(Appendix~\ref{app:hardness-anysize}) points the same way for far larger
budgets of arbitrary-size queries.  We conjecture that predictive greedy is
worst-case optimal within $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{lin}}$ for $n\ge4K^{5}$;
settling the last
$O(1/K^{2})$ is open.
