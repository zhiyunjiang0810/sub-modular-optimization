\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Approximate incremental oracles.}
The closest error model replaces the exact greedy step by an oracle returning
an element whose true marginal gain is within a factor of the best available
one, the $\alpha$-approximate incremental oracle of
\citet{goundan2007revisiting}, whose $\alpha\ge1$ is exactly the selection
error $\etasel$ of Definition~\ref{def:etasel}.  Their Theorem~1 gives
$1-(1-1/(\alpha k))^{k}\ge1-e^{-1/\alpha}$ under such an oracle, which at
$\alpha=1$ is the classical cardinality-constrained bound of
\citet{nemhauser1978analysis}, unimprovable by polynomially many value
queries \citep{nemhauser1978best}; for the related set cover problem no
polynomial-time algorithm approximates within $(1-\varepsilon)\ln n$ unless
$\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{DTIME}(n^{O(\log\log n)})$
\citep{feige1998threshold}.  We differ in the question, not the bound:
Proposition~\ref{prop:guarantee} is their theorem restated in the model of
Section~\ref{sec:model} and attributed as such, while what is new is the
exact worst-case ratio at every error level (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}), a
per-$K$ attaining family at error $\etasel$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:tightness}),
and a matching bounded-query obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:hardness}).

\paragraph{Approximate submodularity.}
A second line weakens the objective instead of the access, measuring distance
from submodularity by the submodularity ratio $\gamma$ of
\citet[Definition~2.3]{das2011submodular}.  Greedy then still returns a
$(1-e^{-\gamma})$ fraction of the optimum \citep[Theorem~3.2]{das2011submodular},
and \citet{elenberg2018restricted} derive weak submodularity from restricted
strong convexity, extending constant-factor greedy guarantees beyond
submodular objectives.  There the oracle is exact and the structure
approximate, here the structure is assumed and the access is not; the third
experiment family of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} lies in the intersection,
its true objective being measurably neither monotone nor submodular.

\paragraph{Noise and inexact oracles.}
A third line perturbs the value oracle itself, by random noise or by allowing
the queried function to be only approximately submodular.
\citet{hassidim2017submodular} recover a ratio arbitrarily close to $1-1/e$
under independent noise once the budget is large enough, and state that
adversarial noise admits no non-trivial guarantee;
\citet[Theorem~3]{horel2016maximization} show that for
$\varepsilon$-approximately submodular functions with $\varepsilon$ above
roughly $n^{-1/2}$ no algorithm with polynomially many queries beats
$O(n^{-\delta})$; and \citet{bhawalkar2025unified} treat a persistent noisy
value oracle, where repeated queries to a set return one value, obtaining
$1-1/e$ for monotone objectives under a matroid constraint, $1/e$ in the
non-monotone matroid case and $1/2$ unconstrained.  Our error is a
deterministic multiplicative band on marginal gains, adversarial inside the
band, so the answer is a finite ratio at every error level rather than either
full recovery or vacuity, and the selection error we state it at is a
property of the finished run.

\paragraph{Learning-augmented algorithms and learn-then-optimize.}
This framework asks for algorithms that improve with better predictions but
do not degrade much when the predictions are poor
\citep{purohit2018improving}, the first property being consistency in its
learning-augmented sense; \citet{lykouris2021competitive} realize it for
caching with a competitive ratio that decreases as the oracle's error
decreases and is capped at $O(\log k)$.  Learning a submodular objective is
itself hard, since PMAC-learning monotone submodular functions under
arbitrary distributions forces approximation factor $\tilde\Omega(n^{1/3})$
\citep{balcan2018submodular}, and against a background of negative results in
which optimizing a learned surrogate lands arbitrarily far from the true
optimum, \citet{rosenfeld2018learning} characterize which surrogates can be
optimized at all.  Predicting other objects does help: a
$1/2-\varepsilon$ expected ratio for dynamic monotone submodular
maximization under predicted insertion and deletion times, with amortized
update time degrading in the prediction error $\eta_{\mathrm{AB}}$
\citep{agarwal2024learning},\footnote{Their $\eta_{\mathrm{AB}}$ counts
elements whose actual insertion or deletion time is far from the predicted
one; it is unrelated to the multiplicative marginal-gain error $\eta$ of
Definition~\ref{def:eta} and is renamed here to keep the two apart.} and
noisy predictions of the optimal solution bypassing classical hardness for
problems such as max-cut \citep{cohenaddad2024learning}.  What is predicted
there is the solution, the schedule, or a function learned once and then
optimized; here the prediction is the objective, queried adaptively during
the run, and the guarantee is stated in an error measure the run reports.

\paragraph{Optimization from samples and query models.}
When only sampled sets and values are available, bounded curvature still
permits a positive result \citep{balkanski2016power}, while in general no
constant-factor approximation follows from polynomially many samples even for
coverage functions learnable to arbitrary accuracy
\citep{balkanski2017limitations}.  We keep adaptive query access and move the
difficulty into the accuracy of the answers, so Theorem~\ref{thm:hardness}
concerns what a bounded number of small queries extracts from an inaccurate
predictor, not what a fixed sample reveals.
